## Post 5 — 2015-10-26
**Title:** Top Signs You Don't Know the First Thing About Optimizing Conversions
**Type:** Article
**Likes:** 8
**URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/top-signs-you-dont-know-first-thing-optimizing-whitney
**Content:**

Transforming your existing site architecture into a conversion machine is tough business. For eCommerce, on average, 98% of sessions to your business will not convert. For publishers, opt-in rates to email are even lower and ad revenue is tough to glean without tremendous traffic scale and audience reach. Here are the top signs that you are currently failing your brand at monetizing the traffic coming into your site: Sending visitors to a page that's not relevant to themConsumers click with specific intent, and your landing page needs to meet their expectations. For example, if a prospect clicks an ad for red boots, he or she expects to see red boots. Not a page with all different types of footwear. Make sure you create unique landing pages for ads that coincide with the consumers' intent to click. Causing too much distraction with your love of visual flairConsumers are easily distracted—it's hard for them to focus on one thing for more than a few seconds at a time. So it's no surprise that simple conversion pages always win—test after test. Save your images, shadowing, decorative fonts, etc., for whitepapers and the like. Conversion rates are higher on simpler, double opt-in submission forms. Combining reciprocity and simplicity can add a new kind of magic to your conversion numbers. To learn other areas of improvement for your conversion rate, check out the full list here at Adweek

**Title:** Building Your Site's Email List: Asking For Opt In Isn’t Good Enough
**Type:** Article
**Likes:** 4
**URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/building-your-sites-email-list-asking-opt-isnt-good-whitney
**Content:**

Copyhackers.com recently worked with Bounce Exchange to increase the performance of their email capture efforts. The results: 4 signups from Bounce Exchange for every 1 that the rest of the site's brought in. With optimization, now a nearly 5:1 ratio. Here is what they had to say about our tactics: "Choice without consequence is no choice at all. When a visitor is presented with an opt-in form, it’s so often the case that said opt-in form has just one button, and that button is there to be clicked if you choose to opt in. If you choose not to opt in, you do not have to click a button to state your preference; you simply X out, click out or otherwise ignore the opt-in button. Most of our opt-ins are active and opt-outs are passive. Stop giving your prospects a choice as if consequences are not built in. As if that makes you a nice guy. Every choice has a consequence. Put the consequence on the page. Make the prospect aware of the consequence so they make a more informed decision." Read More at: http://copyhackers.com/2015/05/choices-consequences-opt-in-boxes/
